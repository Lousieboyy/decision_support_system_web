Quick reference

Finding your way around the DSS Portal

One system, three jobs. This guide covers what each role actually does day to day — not every button, just the ones you'll use.

Admin — verifies & oversees Authority — dispatches work Field Worker — fixes it
01

Welcome

What the Portal is for, in one paragraph.

The DSS Portal is where a citizen's complaint — a pothole, a broken streetlight, an overflowing drain — turns into a job someone actually does, and a record of whether the fix held. Residents report issues from a separate mobile app; everything after that happens here.

The short version of how it flows

A resident reports an issue → an Admin checks it's real and routes it to the right department → an Authority officer at that department sends it to a team → a Field Worker on that team does the repair and submits proof → it gets verified and marked Resolved.

You'll only ever see the parts of this that are yours to act on — the Portal shows a different home screen and menu depending on which of the three roles you're signed in as.

02

Signing in

Getting an account, and what to do if you don't have one yet.

  1. Open the Portal and enter the username and password your admin gave you, then select Sign In to Portal.
  2. No account yet? Switch to the Request Access tab, fill in your name, choose Authority or Worker, pick your department, and submit. An admin has to approve it before you can sign in.
  3. Once approved, sign in normally. If your request is still pending or was turned down, the sign-in screen will tell you.

Forgot your password, or need a new department added? That's an admin task — ask whoever manages your Portal accounts, from the Users page.

Every report starts in your queue as Pending. Open Reports, click a row, and you'll see the photo, the AI's guess at the category, and its confidence.

  1. Check the photo actually shows a real infrastructure problem, and isn't a duplicate of something already reported nearby — the Portal flags likely duplicates for you automatically.
  2. Pick the correct authority from the dropdown (it's already pre-filled with a best guess), add a note if useful, and select Approve & Send. The report moves to In Review and that authority now owns it.
  3. If it's fake, spam, or not a real infrastructure issue, select Reject instead. It closes immediately as Rejected.

Once a worker submits repair proof, the report comes back to you one more time (as In Maintenance, worker-completed) for a final check — confirm the fix, or reject the photo and it returns to the worker.

Manual override

At the bottom of any report's detail view, you can force its status directly — for the rare case a report is stuck or was set wrong by mistake. Use it sparingly; it skips the normal checks the workflow buttons enforce.

Predictive Hotspots

A read-only tab built entirely from already-resolved reports — it's not a work queue. Recurring Failures flags a spot where the same kind of problem came back after being fixed; Systemic flags two different problem types showing up together (like drainage and road damage), which usually means one is causing the other. Found a pattern worth a team checking on their next round? Use Notify Team inside either card — it doesn't reopen any report, it just puts a flag in that team's notification bell.

Any number on this page you can click — a bar, a slice, a zone — opens Find Reports with that exact filter already applied, so you can see the reports behind the figure.

Users & AI Dataset

Two admin-only pages most staff never need to open.

Users

  • Pending Requests — approve or reject self-registered Authority/Worker accounts.
  • All Accounts — every account, active or not; delete an account you no longer need.
  • Create Account — add someone directly, already active, no approval step.
  • Audit Log — a record of logins, approvals and deletions from this browser.

AI Dataset

The photo-recognition model behind category suggestions learns from real submitted photos. This page is where a batch of newly-collected images gets reviewed before joining that training set — approve a correctly-labeled photo, reject a bad one, or fix its label first. Most staff will never need this tab; it exists for whoever maintains the model.

07

Authority — dispatching a report

Once Admin routes a report to your department, it's in your queue as In Review.

  1. Open the report and pick a Team — you'll see each team's current load (open jobs, unclaimed count, worker count) right in the dropdown, so you're not guessing who has room.
  2. Optionally narrow it to one Crew within that team, if the team is split into sub-groups. Leave it blank to drop the job in the whole team's shared pool.
  3. Add a note if the crew needs context, then Send to Crew. The report becomes In Process and sits unclaimed until a worker accepts it.

Once a job is claimed and in progress, you can still Transfer it to a different team entirely, or Move Crew within your own team — both from the report's detail view, under "Team Status".

08

Managing teams & crews

The Teams page — your team's health at a glance, worst-first.

Each team card shows open work, load per worker, how long the oldest unclaimed job has been waiting, and the last 7 days' flow (jobs finished vs. arrived). A colored label — Healthy, Strained, or Bottleneck — is decided by the backend, not guessed, so it means the same thing on every card.

  • Release requests at the top of the page are teams asking to hand a job to someone else — approve (optionally picking who receives it) or deny.
  • Expand a team's card to see its roster, grouped by crew. Create a new crew, add or remove members, mark someone on leave, or disable a whole crew (e.g. everyone's off at once) without deleting it.
09

Field Worker — your jobs

Your home screen is called My Workflow — everything on it is already scoped to you.

Overview1
Map View
Reports

Four numbers greet you every time: active jobs, jobs waiting in your team's pool for anyone to take, how many you've resolved, and your all-time total.

  1. A new job lands in your team's shared pool as In Process, unclaimed. Open it from Reports and select Accept Task — first to accept gets it.
  2. Head to the location and select Start Work. The status becomes In Maintenance.
  3. Finished? Open the report, upload a photo of the completed fix, describe what you did, and select Submit Proof. It now waits on Admin/Authority to verify.

Crew work is shared. If a teammate already claimed a job, you can still open it and select Start Work to help — it's not locked to whoever accepted first.

Proof photo rejected? It's not a dead end — the job returns to In Maintenance so you can retake it and resubmit, using the rejection note as the reason.

12

Questions & answers

Q. I can only see my own department's reports — is that a bug?
No — Authority and Worker accounts are scoped to their own department by design. Admin is the only role that sees everything, and can toggle "All Departments" on Insights.
Q. What does "My Dept Only" on the Reports page do?
It's on by default for Authority/Worker accounts, hiding every other department's reports from the table. Switch it off to browse city-wide (read-only — you still can't act on a report outside your own department).
Q. A hotspot or grade shows "Not enough data" — why?
Every score in the Portal needs a minimum number of reports before it's shown as a real figure, rather than one or two reports masquerading as a trend. Below that threshold, it's reported honestly as insufficient data instead of a misleading number.
Q. Where do I go to see every report matching a specific filter?
Find Reports — reachable by clicking almost any chart, bar, or number on Insights. Every filter in it (date, category, department, zone, status) stays live and combinable, and the results export straight to PDF.
